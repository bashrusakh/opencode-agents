# v28.38

## Claim authority follows provenance, not containers

v28.38 closes a contract-normalization gap that could make agents treat a referenced issue, PR, plan, document, test, comment, or task assignment as a verbatim specification.

The pack now distinguishes the authority of each material claim from the artifact that happens to contain it:

- referencing an artifact establishes context/scope but does not silently adopt every statement inside it;
- a claim becomes an intended outcome/invariant/constraint only when that authority is established from current normalized user intent or applicable project-local authoritative rules; an explicit current instruction may elevate the whole artifact or selected parts;
- claims without established authority remain evidence or hypotheses to verify; evidence can support or refute them but cannot by itself turn them into requirements;
- headings such as acceptance criteria do not self-authenticate the claims beneath them;
- planner/orchestrator normalize claim authority before handoff; reviewer normalizes it before judging the implementation against a stated contract;
- semantic correspondence still applies afterward: technical evidence must justify the semantic conclusion actually being claimed.

This keeps issue-derived work useful without letting proposed mechanisms or derived acceptance wording become a self-confirming implementation contract merely because they were written into the issue.
