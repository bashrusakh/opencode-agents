# v30.85 beta — accountable workflow-owner control plane

## Purpose

v30.85 rebalances the orchestration control plane. The `code-orchestrator` remains non-implementing, but it is now explicitly the accountable owner of the user-authorized engineering outcome rather than a dispatcher whose normal synthesis is delegated to independent controllers.

The release preserves `semantic-checkpoint`, `trajectory-auditor`, and `session-evaluator`, but sharpens their lifecycle questions:

- `code-orchestrator` — first-line whole-task synthesis, model maintenance, and next-action decision;
- `semantic-checkpoint` — independent authority-sensitive judgment only when a material semantic proposition remains unresolved after owner reconciliation (or policy explicitly requires independence);
- `trajectory-auditor` — independent longitudinal second opinion only when bounded owner reassessment leaves a concrete residual trajectory question (or the user explicitly requests one);
- `session-evaluator` — post-hoc diagnosis of both semantic compliance and workflow-owner quality.

## Runtime semantic changes

### `code-orchestrator`

Adds one proportional owner loop before material downstream transitions:

`ingest -> reconcile -> update whole-task model -> decide -> delegate`

The parent must keep specialist evidence, hypotheses, recommendations, unresolved questions, and reported state distinct. A specialist's recommended next role/stage/mechanism/remediation is not a workflow transition until the parent independently reconciles and adopts the required proposition.

The orchestrator may resolve a narrow factual conflict from safe read-only primary evidence when that is cheaper and clearer than spawning another research stage, but this does not grant implementation, deep discovery, debugging, or review ownership.

Longitudinal patterns that previously forced trajectory audit now first force owner reassessment. If that pass yields one evidence-backed causal/ownership/acceptance model, the workflow continues normally. A fresh trajectory audit is used only for a residual question such as two materially plausible models that primary evidence cannot discriminate, a destabilized revised strategy, or unresolved high-impact completion risk.

### `semantic-checkpoint`

The checkpoint remains independent and pre-mutation, but its activation boundary is narrowed. It now gates unresolved authority-bearing semantic propositions, not routine evidence-backed changes to technical owner, fix level, shared abstraction, invariant representation, or necessary implementation scope under already-settled behavior.

Its handoff must state that owner reconciliation was performed, identify the exact unresolved semantic proposition, strongest authority/evidence, mutation boundary, and why the owner could not safely settle the authority relation directly.

### `trajectory-auditor`

The auditor is explicitly a second opinion. Its handoff must state owner reassessment performed, current owner model, materially plausible alternatives, exact unresolved trajectory question, why primary evidence could not resolve it, and the proposed transition being checked. The auditor continues to recover primary session history independently and cannot choose a replacement fix/design.

### `session-evaluator`

The evaluator remains post-hoc. It now additionally checks:

- owner synthesis versus specialist pass-through;
- task-model continuity;
- whether the parent independently determined the next required action;
- whether `semantic-checkpoint`/`trajectory-auditor` were overused as substitutes for ordinary owner reasoning or underused when genuine independent judgment was required.

## Eval / gate changes

- `code-orchestrator` contract audit gains required `owner_synthesis` and `owner_control_plane` capabilities.
- `semantic-checkpoint` gains a required residual-authority activation capability.
- `trajectory-auditor` gains a required second-opinion activation capability.
- `session-evaluator` gains a required workflow-owner-quality capability.
- the `code-orchestrator -> semantic-checkpoint` relation now has its own high-risk handoff profile; high-risk profiles increase from 15 to 16 while the closed-world relation inventory remains 59/59;
- ST-85…ST-96 are rewritten for owner-first behavior, and ST-97…ST-106 cover pass-through, epistemic-class preservation, checkpoint/auditor activation boundaries, whole-task ownership, post-hoc owner audit, bounded direct reconciliation, and anti-think-loop behavior;
- contract selftests now include explicit malicious fixtures for specialist pass-through and controller-first orchestration; both must fail.

## Non-goals / preservation

This release does not make `code-orchestrator` an implementer, reviewer, debugger, planner, or universal repository explorer. It does not remove independent controllers. It changes **when independence adds value**: ordinary owner reasoning stays with the owner; controllers challenge residual uncertainty.

Simple isolated fixes remain lightweight. One bounded owner pass may be only a short reconciliation before direct routing. The release explicitly forbids recursive self-review/confirmation loops.

## Validation claim boundary

Structural, contract, static-regression, handoff, installer, clean-extraction, and targeted mutation-probe evidence may be recorded for this beta. Fresh isolated behavioral replay is a separate evidence class and must not be claimed unless actually run.
